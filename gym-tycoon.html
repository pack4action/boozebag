How it works

Opening up. You start with a Customer Desk and nothing else. Put it on the floor and the doors open: it takes membership fees by itself, and the shop opens with it. Every location needs a desk of its own before it earns a cent, and a desk is never sold.

The money. Gear does not pay you directly. What a machine takes collects in a coin bubble above it, and you tap the bubble to pick it up. A bubble holds a round figure -- about two minutes of that machine's takings, rounded to something worth reading -- and once it is full the machine stops earning until you empty it. Upgrading the Customer Desk raises what every bubble can hold, up to an hour at Mk IV, and hiring Cashiers collects a share for you as it is earned. Three Cashiers and nothing waits on the floor at all.

Placing gear. Click a piece in storage to pick it up, drag it or nudge it with the arrows, then press Place. It stands wherever you left it rather than in a grid square. Click a piece already down to move it again or put it back in storage. Gear in storage sells for 60% of what it cost.

Room to use it. Every machine keeps a strip of clear floor on the side you step onto it from, drawn as a dashed patch while you hold it. Two treadmills side by side are fine; one parked behind another is not, because nobody could get on the back one.

Arrangement. Two pieces of the same category, which are colour-coded, standing within about two metres of each other are worth +12% each. A Gear Fridge or Hype Sound System next to something from a different category is worth +20%.

Fittings. A palm, a water cooler, a mirror wall or a neon sign earns nothing by itself. It raises the room's vibe, which lifts everything else in that room and draws a bigger crowd. Each one takes floor a machine could have had, which is the trade.

Locations and rooms. The Garage, Basement, Rooftop and Boardwalk are four separate gyms, each growing its own chain of rooms joined by hallways, and each laid out differently. Gear stays where you put it when you switch. Drag to pan, and scroll or pinch to zoom.

Levels. Every piece you buy earns experience, and dearer kit earns more. Levelling up is what opens the other locations, the office tier and the staff roles.

Jobs. Three standing requests on the Jobs tab, with the money and experience stated up front. Finish one and another takes its place. From level 3 there is also one timed rush order at a time, worth three times a standing job, with eight minutes to do it in. Miss one and the board goes quiet for twenty minutes.

The counter. A Juice Bar or a Pro Shop earns on the floor like anything else, and it also makes stock. Start a batch on the Counter tab and it runs in real time whether you are watching or not, three back to back. The quick recipe is for filling an order now; the slow one is most of an hour of stock for someone closing the tab. Finished batches keep in the larder, up to 25 of each, and the delivery orders on the Jobs tab pay about double what a standing job pays for them.

Staff. Cashiers collect the takings for you, Cleaners lift every room's vibe, Receptionists make the rush bigger, and a Floor Manager adds to everything. Each extra hire in a role is worth less than the one before while the wage stays the same, so there is a point past which the next one costs more than they bring in. Anyone can be let go.

Rush hours. The gym fills up in the morning and again in the evening, on your own clock, and everything earns a bonus while it lasts. A quiet hour is never worse than normal. An open day is the same thing on demand, once a cooldown allows.

Franchising. Once the shop runs out, cash the whole gym in for points that multiply everything from then on. You keep your level and everything it unlocked.
